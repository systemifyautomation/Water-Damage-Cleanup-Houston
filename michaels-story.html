Homeowner Story

Michael's Story

An overnight burst pipe changed how one Spring Branch family saw their home.

This is Michael's story.

“I've been living in Spring Branch, Houston, with my wife and two children for almost 17 years. We bought our house when our first child was born, and over the years we put a lot of time, money, and love into making it our home.

I never thought I would wake up one morning and see years of hard work being destroyed.

It was a Thursday morning when I woke to the sound of dripping water. At first, I did not think much of it. But when I got out of bed and walked into the hallway, I immediately felt water beneath my feet.

A pipe in our attic had burst during the night, and water had been leaking into our house for hours while we were sleeping. Part of the living-room ceiling had collapsed. The walls were soaked, the hardwood floors were beginning to swell, and our bedroom carpet was completely saturated. There was water everywhere.

I rushed to shut off the main water supply, but by that point the damage was already extensive.

I'm usually the kind of person who fixes things around the house myself. But that morning, I just stood there looking around, completely overwhelmed. My wife was trying to save our family photographs while I was moving furniture away from the damaged areas.

I remember looking at the hardwood floors I had installed myself years earlier and realizing that they probably could not be saved. What scared me most was not knowing how bad the damage actually was. I could see the water on the floor, but I had no idea how much of it was trapped behind the walls or underneath the flooring.

I started worrying about mold, electrical damage, repair costs, and where my family would stay. Your home is supposed to be the one place where you feel safe. Suddenly, I did not even know if it was safe for us to stay inside.

I realized pretty quickly that a mop, some towels, and a few household fans were not going to solve this problem. I contacted a local water damage cleanup service.

When the cleanup professionals arrived, they inspected the affected rooms and used moisture-detection equipment to identify areas where water had traveled behind the walls. Some sections looked perfectly fine from the outside, but there was significant moisture trapped inside.

They walked me through everything. They explained what could be saved and what needed to be removed. Having someone explain what was happening and what we needed to do made a huge difference. I finally felt like I had some control over the situation.

The crew extracted the standing water and moved our belongings into unaffected areas. They removed damaged drywall, soaked insulation, parts of the ceiling, and some of the hardwood flooring. For several days, industrial fans and dehumidifiers ran throughout the house while the technicians checked moisture levels and documented the damage.

It was not an overnight process, but every day I could see things getting a little better.

Once everything was sufficiently dry, reconstruction began. New insulation went in. The drywall was replaced, the ceiling was repaired, the walls were repainted, and new flooring was installed.

I remember walking into the living room after the painting was finished. For the first time in weeks, it actually looked like our home again. Eventually, we moved our furniture back, hung our family photographs on the walls, and returned to our normal routine.

That first evening, my wife and I sat on the couch together. There were no fans running, no exposed walls, and no equipment scattered around the house. Just silence.

I do not think I had ever appreciated that feeling as much as I did that night. Getting that feeling back meant more to us than any of the repairs.”
